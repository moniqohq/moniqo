A budget is only as good as the feedback loop it creates. Reports close that loop — they turn raw transactions into insight you can act on.

## What your spending report actually tells you

The spending breakdown shows where money went across all your envelopes for a given period. The most useful insight isn't the number itself — it's whether it surprised you.

If your "eating out" envelope is consistently overspent, that's not a self-control problem. It's a signal that your allocation is too low for your actual lifestyle, and the envelope needs to be adjusted.

## Month-over-month trends

Comparing this month to last month reveals behavioral patterns. Did you spend more on transport in May than April? Is your grocery envelope creeping up?

Trends tell you when an envelope needs permanent adjustment vs. when a month was just unusually expensive.

## The "To Be Budgeted" indicator

Moniqo surfaces how much income you've received but haven't yet assigned. Keeping this number at zero is the goal of zero-based budgeting. If it's growing, it means unallocated money is sitting idle — money that could be working toward your goals.

Reports aren't about judgment. They're about clarity. The clearer the picture, the better the decisions.
